A real PHOTOGRAPH, indistinguishable from dramatic documentary aviation photography, as if taken on a bright summer afternoon in July 1971 at about 8,500 metres above northern Japan with a modern full-frame camera and a long lens from another aircraft. Clear, thin, very transparent air; a deep cobalt-blue sky; hard, dazzling high-altitude sunlight glinting on polished metal; crisp detail, natural colours, true photographic depth of field. Seen from the side at the same height: a white Boeing 727-200 airliner of the early 1970s — a long narrow fuselage, swept wings, three jet engines grouped at the tail and a tall T-shaped tail — painted plain white with one simple dark-blue stripe along the windows and no markings at all, flying perfectly straight and level from left to right across the middle of the frame, filling most of its width. Close beside it, a little farther from the camera and only a little higher, dangerously close — only a few dozen metres away — is a small silver North American F-86F Sabre jet fighter — bare polished aluminium, a round air intake in the nose, swept-back wings, a bubble canopy, no markings at all — rolled into a steep left bank , its silver underside and swept wings flashing in the sun. Far below, rows of white summer cumulus clouds and, between them, the dark green forested mountains and valleys of rural northern Japan. COMPOSITION, critical: 16:9 frame. Very large lettering will later cover the top 15% and the bottom 20% of the frame, and one huge word will run across the whole middle, so the picture will mostly be seen through the gaps between its letters. So both aircraft must be LARGE, spread across the middle band of the frame, readable at a glance even when the picture is tiny. Keep plain deep-blue sky at the top and the cloud tops and mountains at the bottom. Original composition: do not copy any existing photograph, painting or film scene.
